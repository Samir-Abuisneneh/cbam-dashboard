# CBAM Corridor Cost Model

Deterministic techno-economic model comparing the carbon compliance cost of
hydrogen and ammonia along two maritime corridors under two carbon regimes.

- **Halifax to Hamburg**: EU CBAM, EU ETS Maritime, FuelEU Maritime.
- **Ningbo to Felixstowe**: UK CBAM (from 2027), UK ETS Maritime. No FuelEU.

Student 3 deliverable (Samir Abuisneneh), MSc Data Science for Business,
University of Bristol, 2026.

**Results are not in this file.** They live in the dissertation. This README
covers what the model is, how to run it, where the numbers come from and what it
assumes. Every figure it produces is regenerated by running it.

## Running it

```
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt -r requirements-dev.txt
.venv/bin/jupyter lab run_model.ipynb   # the main run
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q           # the test suite
.venv/bin/ruff check .                  # lint, configured in pyproject.toml
.venv/bin/streamlit run dashboard.py    # interactive scenario explorer
```

`requirements.txt` is the Streamlit Cloud deployment set and is deliberately
minimal. `requirements-dev.txt` adds matplotlib, pytest, ruff and the notebook
and PDF build tooling.

`run_model.ipynb` is the entry point and produces everything end to end. Outputs
land in `cbam_model/outputs/`.

`dashboard.py` is a Streamlit UI over the same scenario matrix. It calls
`cbam_model` live rather than reading CSVs, so it cannot drift from the tested
regulatory logic, and it accepts only the two corridors covered by the test
suite. Built as a client-facing tool; since the industry partner withdrew on
6 August 2026 it is internal, used to generate figures for the dissertation and
the defence.

The library is `.py` and the entry point is `.ipynb` because notebooks cannot be
imported as modules. The notebook imports `cbam_model/`, and so does the test
suite. That split is what makes the regulatory logic testable at all.
`build_notebooks.py` regenerates the notebook and is tooling, not model.

## Where the numbers come from

**Regulatory values** are in `config/regulatory_constants.py`, each with its
source and, where it matters, the reasoning behind choosing it. That file is
long on purpose: it is the audit trail. If you want to know why a number is what
it is, it is there rather than here.

**Maritime inputs** come from Gayu (Student 2): distances, vessel
specifications, fuel burn, voyage CO2e and the resulting EU ETS, UK ETS and
FuelEU costs. `validation/gayu_reproduction.py` holds her published figures as
literal expected values, so divergence surfaces as a test failure rather than
quiet drift. Her figures supersede the build spec wherever they disagree. The
largest correction was the Atlantic distance: 2,962 nm against the spec's 6,300.

**Emissions and production costs** come from Riya (Student 1). Embedded
emissions are anchored on the CBAM regulatory default value where one exists,
with literature pathways bracketing it as sensitivities.

**Cargo tonnage** joins the two layers. An 84,000 m3 carrier at the IGC Code 98%
filling limit gives 56,142 t of ammonia or 5,828 t of liquid hydrogen, a ratio of
9.6 to 1. That ratio drives the hydrogen-versus-ammonia comparison on its own,
since each tonne of hydrogen absorbs 9.6 times more of a voyage's cost.

**Currencies are never converted.** EU figures stay in EUR, UK in GBP, matching
how Gayu presents her tables. The one exception is inside the sourcing
optimisation, where a single market's objective function needs one currency.

## The three decisions that shape every result

Each is argued in full in the code. Summarised here so a reader knows they exist.

**1. The free allocation adjustment nets off a benchmark.** The obligation is
`max(0, embedded - CBAM_benchmark x (1 - CBAM_factor) x CSCF)`, not embedded
emissions scaled by the CBAM factor. Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2620,
adopted under Article 31(2), sets the calculation out directly. Both readings
stay implemented and `analysis.outputs.cbam_mechanism_comparison` reports them
side by side, so the size of the choice stays visible.

**2. The CBAM benchmark is not the EU ETS benchmark.** IR 2025/2620 defines a
distinct one: ammonia 1.522 either way, hydrogen 5.089 against the ETS 7.98.
Using the ETS figure shielded hydrogen by 56.8% more than the law allows.

**3. Canada's origin price is what a producer effectively pays.** Article 9
allows a deduction for the price *effectively paid*, and Regulation (EU)
2025/2083 requires any rebate reducing it to be taken into account. Under Nova
Scotia's output-based system a facility pays only on emissions above its
performance standard, 4% of them in 2026 rising to 8% by 2030. The deduction is
therefore EUR 2.37 in 2026, not the EUR 59.27 headline rate. Nova Scotia's
published outturn corroborates the order of magnitude at 8.2% in 2023 and 10.0%
in 2024. Pass `basis="observed"` or `basis="headline"` to
`origin_carbon_price_canada_eur` to reproduce either.

## What is assumed rather than sourced

- **CSCF held at 1.0.** No 2026 value published. `CBAM_CSCF_IS_SOURCED` is False
  and the outputs carry it.
- **Canadian facilities perform at their own baseline.** One running cleaner
  pays nothing and earns credits; one running dirtier pays on more.
- **UK CBAM default emissions values.** HMRC has not published them, so the EU
  country-specific figure stands in. This probably overstates UK liability on
  the China corridor.
- **The UK ETS price input.** SI 2026/809 specifies a quarterly mean of auction
  clearing prices. The model uses an annual mean of UKA futures settlements,
  because the statutory series is not freely available: ICE report 278 is
  subscription-only and DESNZ publishes annually in arrears. Checked 15 August
  2026. A defensible approximation, not the statutory quantity.
- **Per-pathway supply limits in the sourcing optimisation.** No plant, terminal
  or fleet figure was available, so results are swept across a range rather than
  reported at a single value.
- **The EUA daily price series** comes from a commercial aggregator with no
  published methodology. Confined to the forecasting module, whose output never
  reaches a corridor result.
- **Conversion and freight cost** are a declared scope boundary, not a pending
  input. The study reports carbon compliance cost per tonne, not delivered cost.
  Both terms are invariant to production pathway, so they cancel out of any
  within-corridor comparison.
- **The hydrogen vessel is a counterfactual.** An ammonia carrier runs at about
  -33 C and cannot hold liquid hydrogen at -253 C. Holding the vessel constant
  isolates the effect of cargo density. It is not a shipping option available
  today, and boil-off is not modelled.

## Superseded figures, do not requote

Three corrections changed results after figures had already circulated. Anything
in a document predating the relevant date is stale.

| Date | What changed | What it superseded |
|---|---|---|
| 7 Aug 2026 | Fertiliser default-value mark-up is 1%, not 10/20/30 | All ammonia EU figures |
| 8 Aug 2026 | CBAM benchmark, not EU ETS benchmark | All hydrogen EU figures; hydrogen's corridor crossover and lock-in reversal |
| 15 Aug 2026 | Canada origin price is the effective rate, not the headline | All Halifax-Hamburg figures; the corridor crossover, lock-in and the live switching decision |

Two findings no longer exist and must not be cited: the **corridor crossover**
and **lock-in**. Both were artefacts of over-shielding the EU corridor. The tests
that pinned them now pin their absence, so an input change that reinstates
either fails loudly rather than quietly restoring a dead claim.

## One thing to re-check before submission

IR 2025/2620 recital 10 says the 2026 CBAM benchmarks are based on *estimated*
2026-2030 ETS benchmarks, to be reviewed within a month of the final ones
publishing, with updated values applying from 1 January 2027. IR 2026/1412
published those on 29 June 2026. No amending regulation was found as of 15
August 2026. If one appears, the 2027-2030 hydrogen benchmark moves and every
corridor result with it.

## Layout

```
cbam_model/
  config/
    regulatory_constants.py   every law-derived value, each with its source
    unresolved.py             sentinel for values that must not be guessed
    scenarios.py              the scenario matrix
    vessel_logistics.py       Gayu's vessels, distances and fuel model
  model/
    cbam.py                   EU and UK CBAM
    ets_maritime.py           EU and UK maritime ETS
    fueleu.py                 FuelEU penalty and the RFNBO reward factor
    switching.py              corridor lock-in under asset specificity
    total_cost.py             the two layers and the compliance join
  market_data/                EUA daily price series and its ingest
  forecasting/                carbon price forecasting and its validation
  optimisation/               least-cost sourcing, solved per destination market
  validation/
    unit_checks.py            data contract enforcement
    gayu_reproduction.py      pins every one of Gayu's published figures
    reference_case.py         cross-check against Ramsook et al. (2025)
  analysis/
    sensitivity.py            one-at-a-time sweep and driver ranking
    outputs.py                tables and charts
  data/                       inputs; see data/README.md
tests/
run_model.ipynb
```

**Functional units.** Maritime layer is cost per voyage, in the regime's own
currency. CBAM layer is cost per tonne of product, since embedded emissions are
expressed per tonne. The compliance layer joins them per tonne using cargo
tonnage.

## What the tests protect

292 tests. They pin the facts this project has already got wrong once each:

- The CBAM factor is 2.5% in 2026, not 97.5%.
- UK ETS ignores the international voyage. A 10,000 tCO2 ocean crossing changes
  the Felixstowe cost by nothing.
- The proposed UK ETS extension is opt-in and cannot be selected before 2028.
- The default-value mark-up applies only to the `cbam_default` pathway, and
  `default_value_markup` requires the product rather than defaulting it, because
  a default would silently restore a bug whose output looks plausible.
- No benchmark-based CBAM figure can be produced without the
  `benchmark_is_current` flag travelling with it, and the benchmark mechanism
  refuses to run without an explicit benchmark rather than defaulting to zero.

Expected values are hand-calculated in the test bodies rather than copied from a
previous run. Where a correction changed a result, the test docstring records the
superseded figure so it cannot be requoted by accident.

## Three corrections made to the build spec

1. **The origin carbon price adjustment had a units error.** The spec subtracted
   a EUR-per-tonne price from a EUR total, which can return a negative cost from
   a positive liability. Under Article 9 the origin price reduces the
   certificates surrendered, so it scales with the same emissions and the same
   factor as the obligation.
2. **The FuelEU penalty was missing a divisor.** Annex IV Part B divides the
   compliance balance by GHGIE_actual x 41,000. Since GHGIE_actual is around 90
   gCO2e/MJ, the spec version overstated the penalty by roughly ninety times.
3. **The FuelEU target schedule stopped short of the scenario matrix.** The spec
   carried only the 2025-2029 target, but the matrix runs 2030, where Article
   4(2) tightens the reduction from 2% to 6%.
