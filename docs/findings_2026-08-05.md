## 1. Maritime CO2e (Gayu's update)

**Status: done.**

From 1 January 2026, EU ETS maritime scope expands beyond CO2 to cover CH4
and N2O, on a CO2e basis (EMSA's own ETS extension page). The UK ETS maritime
extension mirrors this from 1 July 2026 with identical factors (SI 2026/392,
Schedule 2A). Gayu's updated notebook adds this as a new section (5b,
"This section is new") and it moves the two headline maritime carbon costs:

| Figure | CO2-only (previous) | CO2e (current) |
|---|---|---|
| EU ETS, Halifax-Hamburg, mid price | EUR 43,208 | **EUR 43,880** |
| UK ETS, Ningbo-Felixstowe, official price | GBP 2,891 | **GBP 2,936** |

Distances, speed, engine assumptions and the FuelEU penalty are unchanged.

**Implementation.** `vessel_logistics.voyage_co2e_tonnes` and
`port_co2e_tonnes` compute CO2 + CH4(GWP 28) + N2O(GWP 265) from IMO
MEPC.391(81) factors, matching Gayu's rounding exactly. `corridor_profile`
now returns `voyage_co2e_t` and `port_in_port_emissions_co2e_t` alongside the
CO2-only figures (kept for reference). `total_cost.maritime_cost_per_voyage`
and `analysis/sensitivity.py` now feed CO2e into the EU/UK ETS cost
functions. `validation/gayu_reproduction.py` reproduces both the CO2-only and
CO2e gas-carrier figures exactly.

**One thing to flag rather than fix.** The container-ship notebook has no
equivalent published update. Its EU ETS/UK ETS expected costs in
`gayu_reproduction.py` are therefore *derived* - the same public IMO formula
applied to the container ship's own fuel burn (EUR 64,744 / GBP 14,511,
against a pre-CO2e EUR 63,752 / GBP 14,288) - not transcribed from a Gayu
notebook. Flagged in the module rather than presented as reproducing a
figure she published, since she hasn't published one for this vessel type.

## 2. Ayub et al. (2024): a second Canada hydrogen cost cross-check

**Status: added as a robustness check, not adopted as the primary figure.**

Riya found: Ayub, H.M.U., Alnouri, S.Y., Stijepovic, M., Stijepovic, V. and
Hussein, I.A. (2024) 'A cost comparison study for hydrogen production between
conventional and renewable methods', *Process Safety and Environmental
Protection*, 186, pp. 1379-1395. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psep.2024.04.080

It reports grey/blue/green hydrogen production cost per kg across five
regions including Canada, which overlaps the Halifax-Hamburg corridor. This
is now a third source alongside the primary literature figure (Riya's
"Production Costs - Literature" sheet) and the existing IEA robustness check,
for the three Canada hydrogen pathways this model uses:

| Pathway | Primary (Riya) | IEA 2025 | IEA 2030 | Ayub (2024) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grey (NG reforming) | EUR 614.5/t | EUR 1,360.6/t | EUR 1,404.5/t | **EUR 614.5/t** |
| Blue (NG reforming + CCUS) | EUR 1,053.4/t | EUR 1,799.5/t | EUR 1,843.4/t | EUR 798.8/t |
| Green (electrolysis) | EUR 3,607.8/t | EUR 5,266.9/t | EUR 3,950.1/t | EUR 9,480.3/t |

(USD converted to EUR at the 23 July 2026 ECB reference rate, same convention
as the rest of the model.)

**Grey hydrogen: the primary figure and Ayub agree exactly** (USD 700/t
both, from different studies) - two independent sources landing on the same
number, which is corroboration rather than a gap.

**Blue hydrogen: no two of the three sources agree**, spanning EUR
798.8-1,843.4/t (a 2.3x range). Ayub sits below the primary figure; IEA sits
well above both.

**Green hydrogen (electrolysis) is the largest and most consequential
spread**: EUR 3,607.8-9,480.3/t (2.6x). Ayub's figure is the high outlier of
the three, not just relative to the primary source but relative to IEA too.
Worth understanding why before treating it as a correction: Ayub's Canada
electrolysis cost is built from a generic industrial electricity price
(USD 0.192/kWh, Statista 2023) rather than a renewable-specific PPA rate,
while the primary figure comes from a study specifically modelling
wind-driven electrolysis in Canada (S0960148125012959) - a materially cheaper
power source than grid-average industrial electricity. The three-way spread
looks less like disagreement about the technology and more like disagreement
about what electricity price a green hydrogen project would actually pay,
which is itself worth stating as a finding rather than resolving by picking
a source.

**Why this wasn't adopted as the primary figure.** Ayub covers Canada and
hydrogen only - no China, no ammonia - so it cannot replace the IEA check,
which covers every corridor/product/pathway combination under one
methodology. It is also, like IEA, a literature/agency figure rather than a
corridor-specific quote for EverWind, the same limitation the primary source
already carries. Added alongside IEA in `data_io.ayub_production_costs()`
rather than swapped in.

**Implementation.** `data_io.ayub_production_costs()`, following the same
shape and "ROBUSTNESS CHECK ONLY" labelling convention as
`iea_production_costs()`. Covered by
`test_ayub_costs_cover_only_canada_hydrogen` and
`test_ayub_grey_hydrogen_matches_the_primary_figure` in `tests/test_model.py`.

against the Ayub figures the way it already runs against IEA, to see whether

source the way it survived the second. Left open rather than run

`docs/findings_2026-08-04.md`).
