## Cited in the section

### 1. Zetterberg (2014) **[V]**

> Zetterberg, L. (2014) 'Benchmarking in the European Union Emissions Trading System: Abatement incentives', *Energy Economics*, 43, pp. 218–224. doi:10.1016/j.eneco.2014.03.002

Author affiliation: IVL Swedish Environmental Research Institute, Stockholm.

Verified from the publisher PDF. Header reads `Energy Economics 43 (2014) 218–224`.

**Used for two claims:**
- Output-based allocation with updated output and *ex-ante* benchmarks "provides as high abatement incentives as auction, but constitutes a production subsidy". Note the condition: with *updated* benchmarks instead, incentives fall somewhat.
- Because benchmarking is conditioned on production, it protects production from leakage. He calls this "the most compelling reason for choosing benchmarking".

### 3. Fischer and Fox (2007) **[V]**

> Fischer, C. and Fox, A.K. (2007) 'Output-Based Allocation of Emissions Permits for Mitigating Tax and Trade Interactions', *Land Economics*, 83(4), pp. 575–599. doi:10.3368/le.83.4.575

Verified from the Land Economics publisher page.

**Used for the subsidy effect:** additional output generates additional allowances, so output-based allocation discourages conservation and shifts abatement toward costlier rate reduction.

**Important, do not cite one-sidedly.** Their overall conclusion is favourable to output-based allocation: the implicit output subsidies mitigate interactions with existing taxes and can produce higher welfare than grandfathering. The section reflects this. If you shorten the sentence, keep the welfare clause.

### 4. Ramsook, Boodlal and Maharaj (2025) **[V]**

> Ramsook, D.A., Boodlal, D. and Maharaj, R. (2025) 'Navigating carbon border adjustments: a case study analyzing the impacts and strategies for Trinidad and Tobago's ammonia exports', *European Journal of Energy Research*, 5(6), pp. 1–11. doi:10.24018/ejenergy.2025.5.6.177

**Used for:**
- Benchmark value: EF_CBAM = 1.57 tCO2/tNH3, their Equation 6
- Average CBAM burden of approximately 22% of export value under business as usual
- 115 USD/tNH₃ average across the horizon, against an assumed FOB price of 535 USD/tNH₃

**Caution.** The claim in the section that "factor-scaling does not reproduce that figure" is a reconstruction, not something stated in their paper. Reconstructing their horizon average at ~2.4 tCO₂/t and ~€75/t gives roughly €116/t under the benchmark form against about €82/t under factor-scaling; they publish 115. That supports the claim but it is inference. Their actual equations have not been read.

### 5. Clausing, Colmer, Hsiao and Wolfram (2026) **[V]**

> Clausing, K., Colmer, J., Hsiao, A. and Wolfram, C. (2026) *The Global Effects of Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanisms*. Working paper, 14 July 2026.

Institutions: UCLA, University of Virginia, Stanford, MIT.

**This is an unpublished working paper.** Check for a published version before submission, and cite as a working paper if none exists.

**Used for the method:** they compute adjusted prices by "netting out free allocations and assessing carbon pricing only on Scope 1 emissions that exceed free-allocation benchmarks", using separate benchmarks for aluminium and steel that distinguish primary from secondary production.

**Also available if needed:** they model CBAM coalitions, finding that without a border adjustment carbon taxes are strategic substitutes, and with one they become strategic complements. They treat Switzerland and the UK as keeping their own schemes separate from the EU ETS, relevant if anyone challenges the "second border regime" gap claim.

### 7. House of Commons Library (2026) **[V]**

> Burnett, N., Dillon, A., Hewitt, T. and Jozepa, I. (2026) *Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism*. House of Commons Library Research Briefing CBP-9935, 10 April 2026.

Verified from the briefing PDF.

**Used for:**
- "There will be no purchase or trading of emissions certificates."
- "The carbon price applied under CBAM will adjust for free allowances and other reductions to the carbon price paid domestically (for instance, under the ETS), and will account for explicit carbon prices in other jurisdictions."
- Start date of 1 January 2027, stated in six places.

**Also in the briefing, for Methodology or Limitations:** inclusion of indirect emissions (Scope 2 and precursor) within the UK CBAM is delayed until 2029, pending the Energy Intensive Industries Compensation Scheme. This confirms the 2029 assumption already in the model. The briefing does not mention Carbon Price Support anywhere.

## Not cited in the section, but verified and needed later

These came out of the Official Journal directly and belong in Methodology.

### Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2620 **[V]**

> Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2620 of 16 December 2025 laying down rules for the application of Regulation (EU) 2023/956 as regards the calculation of the free allocation adjustment.

Annex point 5 table, read from the OJ PDF:

```
2804 10 00   Hydrogen              5,089    5,089
2814 10 00   Anhydrous ammonia     1,522    1,522
```

Recital 17 (steel only, and the basis for saying the Commission does not explain the hydrogen case):

> The rules for the exchangeability of fuel and electricity have been removed for the determination of free allocation under the EU ETS starting in 2026. This means that free allocation granted under some ETS product benchmarks in the steel sector will cover indirect emissions to a certain extent. As the CBAM scope currently only covers direct emissions in the steel sector, only the direct emission share of the respective ETS benchmarks should be considered when determining the corresponding CBAM benchmarks.

Recital 15, which supports "a design CBAM adapts rather than replaces":

> In order to reflect the EU ETS benchmarks, CBAM benchmarks for simple goods were calculated using the EU ETS product benchmarks of the same type of good.

Recital 10, for Limitations, the values are provisional:

> the CBAM benchmarks in 2026 should be based on the estimated ETS benchmarks to be applied in the period from 2026 to 2030 [...] These CBAM benchmarks should be reviewed at the latest one month after the final EU ETS benchmarks for the period from 2026 to 2030 are published. The updated CBAM benchmarks [...] should apply to goods imported from 1 January 2027.

Hydrogen appears nowhere in the recitals.
